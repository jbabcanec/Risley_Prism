\title{Frequency-Lattice Inversion of Multi-Prism Risley Systems:\\
Exact Parameter Recovery with Information-Theoretic Certificates}

\author{J.~Babcanec}
\affiliation{Department of Mathematics, Benedict College, Columbia, SC 29204, USA}
\author{B.~Campbell}
\affiliation{Department of Engineering, Robert Morris University, Moon Township, PA 15108, USA}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We solve the full parameter-recovery inverse problem for multi-prism
Risley beam-steering systems: given one observed scan pattern and
nothing else, recover the complete physical specification---rotation
speeds, wedge angles, glass indices, system geometry, and beam source
($4P+6$ parameters for $P$ prisms; $18$ for the reference case
$P{=}3$).  The key structural fact is that the pattern's analytic
signal $z=x+iy$ is quasi-periodic on the rank-$P$ integer lattice
generated by the \emph{signed} prism speeds, and every quantity the
inverse problem needs is a property of that lattice model: the
generators are the speeds (sign included), the phases of the
fundamental lines are the wedge orientations exactly, and their
amplitudes determine the wedge magnitudes.  The resulting constructive
inversion---matrix-pencil line estimation, CLEAN-style line growth,
integer-coverage basis identification, variable projection, and one
trust-region completion---contains no enumeration and no training: no
sign combinations, no frequency-triple search, no angle grids, no
learned components.  Every answer is \emph{certified}: per-parameter
error bounds derived from the exact-model Jacobian, or a
Fisher-information impossibility statement naming the
deficit---pair resolvability, prism detectability, lattice degeneracy,
mask budget, front-end capacity---together with the observation time
that cures it.  We further derive a rigorous scaling theory in the
prism count: per-instance cost is polynomial in $P$; pairwise speed
crowding demands observation time $\Theta(P^{2})$, and accidental
lattice relations demand time polynomial of degree equal to the
working relation order, yielding a computable feasibility frontier.
Every statistical claim is subjected to a pre-registered large-scale
computational campaign---order $10^{6}$ random configurations under an
adaptive observation protocol and order $10^{7}$ ground-truth
certificate checks (Sec.~\ref{sec:campaign}); pending values are marked
\PH{$\cdot$} throughout.
This is a simulation study; experimental validation is planned.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Background}

Risley prism systems---assemblies of independently rotating optical wedge
prisms that deflect a laser beam without translating the source---were first
described by Risley in 1889~\cite{Risley1889} and have since found
applications in lidar~\cite{Amirault1985}, laser materials
processing~\cite{Dausinger2000,Foehl2003}, infrared
countermeasures~\cite{Bos2007}, free-space optical communications, and
medical laser systems.  A comprehensive review is given by
Garc\'ia-Torales~\cite{GarciaTorales2022}; the 125-year history is
surveyed by Carter and Carter~\cite{Carter2006}.

A physical wedge prism is a solid piece of glass with two non-parallel
planar faces.  A beam entering through the first (entry) face refracts by
Snell's law, propagates through the glass, and refracts again on exit.
Each prism thus contributes \emph{two} refractive interfaces rotating in
lockstep; a system of $P$ prisms has $2P$ interfaces.  The
\emph{forward} problem---predicting the scan pattern from known
parameters---is well understood, both paraxially
\cite{Shaomin1985,Yang2008,Li2011} and by exact ray
tracing~\cite{Marshall1999,Schitea2013,Duma2021}.

\subsection{The inverse problem}

The \emph{parameter-recovery} inverse problem has received far less
attention, and what little exists solves a fundamentally different
problem.  What the literature calls the Risley ``inverse problem''
almost universally denotes \emph{beam steering}: given \emph{known}
hardware, compute the prism orientations that place the beam on a
target---solved in closed form for two prisms in the paraxial
limit~\cite{Yang2008}, by look-up table~\cite{Ostaszewski2006}, and most
recently by neural networks~\cite{Li2024,Yan2026}.  This is a pointing
problem, not an identification problem: the hardware is an input, not an
output.

A smaller line of work does estimate hardware, but only as bounded
corrections to a \emph{known design}.  Li \emph{et al.}~\cite{Li2017}
identify six error parameters of a two-prism pointer (wedge angles,
indices, installation angles) as small deviations around a known
nominal, from controlled pointing measurements on an experimental
platform, by genetic search, improving static pointing from 33 to under
1\,arcsec; pointing-error compensation on an assembled
unit~\cite{BravoMedina2017} and target-free self-calibration of prism
main-section orientation from the Fourier spectrum~\cite{LiZhou2021}
likewise recover partial, near-nominal quantities for two
prisms---never the wedge magnitudes, indices, and rotation dynamics
together, and never blind.

Our problem is different in kind: nothing is nominal and nothing is
controlled.  A time series of workpiece positions is passively observed,
and the \emph{complete} physical specification---rotation speeds
(dynamics, absent from every calibration setting above), both
wedge-angle components per prism, glass indices, layout distances, and
the beam source---is recovered blind, to near machine precision, with
certified error bounds.  For this full system-identification
problem---blind, from a single passively observed pattern, for
$P\ge2$ prisms---no prior solution has been reported.

The difficulty is threefold: the map is nonlinear (iterated Snell
refraction at $2P$ interfaces); the parameter space has $4P+6$
dimensions ($18$ for $P{=}3$); and the problem is severely
ill-conditioned---the Jacobian condition number
$\kappa\approx5\times10^{5}$ means geometry parameters are
roughly five orders of magnitude less sensitive than speeds
(Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}).  In our own earlier work the missing
piece was never the local optimizer---a trust-region method on the
exact model reaches near machine precision from any initialization in the
correct basin---but the \emph{global} stage that finds that basin.
Successive attempts based on FFT peak picking required layers of brute
force to survive their own failure modes: an enumeration of candidate
frequency triples (the top spectral peaks are often harmonics or
intermodulation lines rather than fundamentals), a $2^P$ enumeration of
speed signs (real-valued spectra cannot see rotation direction), and a
grid over wedge-angle space (bin-limited spectral phases are corrupted
by $\bigO(90^\circ)$ discretization error, so angles could not be read
from the spectrum).  This paper removes all three layers, not by better
search, but by identifying the algebraic structure that makes search
unnecessary.  Figure~\ref{fig:hero} shows the end result on one
representative random instance: a dense multi-lobed scan pattern, the lattice-valued
line spectrum the method reads, and the reproduction from the
recovered 18-parameter vector---indistinguishable from the observation
at MSE $2\times10^{-26}$, obtained in $2.9$\,s.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Structure theory} (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}).  Away from an
      explicitly characterized total-internal-reflection (TIR) set, the
      analytic signal of the scan pattern is quasi-periodic on the
      integer lattice generated by the \emph{signed} speeds
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}); the fundamental line of prism $i$
      sits at signed frequency $N_i$ and dominates its conjugate
      (Prop.~\ref{prop:leak}); its phase equals the wedge orientation
      $\alpha_{y,i}$ exactly up to an exact discrete symmetry
      (Props.~\ref{prop:flip}--\ref{prop:phase}); and its amplitude is
      an odd cubic in $\tan\alpha_{x,i}$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:amp}).  A
      finer-lattice lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:finer}) explains why
      residual-guided model selection provably cannot identify the
      generators, forcing the arithmetic selection we use.
\item \textbf{An enumeration-free constructive inversion}
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}): matrix pencil~\cite{HuaSarkar1990,
      RoyKailath1989} line estimation, CLEAN-style~\cite{Hogbom1974}
      line growth at lattice order one, integer-coverage basis
      identification, variable-projection~\cite{GolubPereyra1973}
      lattice refinement, spectral angle readout, and a single
      trust-region completion~\cite{Branch1999} with a deterministic,
      MSE-verified candidate ladder.
\item \textbf{Certificates} (Sec.~\ref{sec:certify}).  Every instance
      returns either all $4P+6$ parameters with per-parameter error
      bounds from the exact-model Jacobian, or a quantitative
      impossibility statement from the Fisher information of the fitted
      lattice model, with the observation time $T$ that would cure it.
      Bound coverage and tightness are measured against ground truth at
      campaign scale (Sec.~\ref{sec:campaign}, E2), and the
      prescriptions are validated by bisection (E4).
\item \textbf{Scaling theory for arbitrary prism count}
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:scaling}).  Exact and asymptotic laws for the
      $4P+6$-dimensional problem: per-instance cost polynomial in $P$;
      an exact pair-crowding law forcing observation time
      $\Theta(P^{2})$; anti-concentration and pigeonhole bounds for
      accidental lattice relations giving a degree-$K$ polynomial
      demand at working relation order $K$; a capacity threshold; and
      the resulting feasibility frontier
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}), tested head-on by the campaign's
      $P\times T$ experiment (E5).
\item \textbf{Tested assumptions at scale.}  Every mathematical claim
      used by the method is verified by a named experiment (A1--A8,
      Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}) run over the campaign ensemble,
      including a direct measurement of the trust-region basin that
      retires the folklore of \textquotedblleft narrow
      basins\textquotedblright\ for this problem.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Physical Prism Model}
\label{sec:forward}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{System geometry}

We consider $P$ rotating wedge prisms along the optical axis $z$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}).  Each prism~$i$ has refractive index
$n_{\mathrm{g},i}$ and two planar faces: a flat \textbf{entry face}
(interface $2i{-}1$, perpendicular to $z$), and an \textbf{exit face}
(interface $2i$) tilted by the wedge angle pair
$(\alpha_{x,i},\alpha_{y,i})$, where $\alpha_x$ is the tilt magnitude
and $\alpha_y$ the azimuthal orientation offset.  Both faces rotate
rigidly at speed $N_i$ (Hz),
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_i(t) = 360\,N_i\,t \pmod{360^\circ},
  \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
and the refractive indices alternate along the path,
$n_0=1,\,n_1=n_{\mathrm{g},1},\,n_2=1,\dots$, so every interface has a
nontrivial Snell ratio.  The beam originates at $(r_x,r_y,0)$ with
angles $(\theta_x^{(0)},\theta_y^{(0)})$; inter-element distances are
source-to-first-face $d_0$, prism thickness $\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$,
inter-prism gap $\delta$, and last-face-to-workpiece $d_W$.  Of these,
$d_0$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$ are held fixed at known nominal values and
are not recovered; the unknown vector for $P{=}3$ is
\begin{align}
  \vect{\theta} = [\,&N_1,N_2,N_3,\ \alpha_{x,1..3},\ \alpha_{y,1..3},
  \ n_{\mathrm{g},1..3},\nonumber\\
  &d_W,\ \delta,\ \theta_x^{(0)},\theta_y^{(0)},\ r_x,r_y\,]
  \in\mathbb{R}^{18}.
  \label{eq:theta}
\end{align}

\subsection{Refraction and rotation}

At interface~$k$ with effective tilt $\varphi_k$ the direction is
updated by vector Snell's law in per-axis reduced form (the $x$ and $y$
projections are traced independently; cross-coupling is
$\bigO(\alpha_x\alpha_y)$, $\sim\!7\%$ relative at $\alpha=15^\circ$,
and exact for the self-consistent inversion used here): with
$\vect{n}=[\tan\varphi,0,-1]^{\top}$,
$\vect{s}=[\tan\theta,0,1]^{\top}$, $\eta=n_{k-1}/n_k$,
\begin{equation}
  \vect{s}_\mathrm{f} =
  \eta\,(\uvec{N}\times(-\uvec{N}\times\uvec{s}_\mathrm{i}))
    - \uvec{N}\,\sqrt{1-\eta^2\,|\uvec{N}\times\uvec{s}_\mathrm{i}|^2}\,.
  \label{eq:snell}
\end{equation}
Rotation enters only through the exit-face normal
\begin{equation}
  \vect{n}_i = \begin{bmatrix}
    \cos(\gamma_i+\alpha_{y,i})\,\tan\alpha_{x,i} \\
    \sin(\gamma_i+\alpha_{y,i})\,\tan\alpha_{x,i} \\
    -1
  \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:normal} is the algebraic seed of the whole method:
$\alpha_{y,i}$ appears \emph{only} as a phase offset added to
$\gamma_i$, and $\alpha_{x,i}$ \emph{only} through the tilt magnitude
$\tan\alpha_{x,i}$.  Two exact consequences follow.

\begin{proposition}[Exact flip symmetry]
\label{prop:flip}
The forward map is invariant under
$(\alpha_{x,i},\alpha_{y,i})\mapsto(-\alpha_{x,i},\alpha_{y,i}+180^\circ)$
for any prism, at all orders.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\cos(\gamma+\alpha_y+180^\circ)\tan(-\alpha_x)
 =\cos(\gamma+\alpha_y)\tan\alpha_x$ and likewise for the sine
component, so the normal~\eqref{eq:normal} is pointwise unchanged; the
trace depends on the parameters only through $\vect{n}_i(t)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Test A1} verifies the invariance to floating-point
precision over the campaign ensemble: $\max|\Delta F| = \PH{A1}$
(Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}).

Because the admissible box $|\alpha_y|\le18^\circ$ contains exactly one
representative of each symmetry class, the flip is a
reparameterization, not a non-uniqueness; it does, however, control the
branch logic of the spectral angle readout
(Sec.~\ref{sec:angleread}).

\begin{proposition}[TIR set]
\label{prop:tir}
The trace is $C^\infty$ in $(\vect{\theta},t)$ except on the set where
$1-\eta^2|\uvec{N}\times\uvec{s}_\mathrm{i}|^2 = 0$ at some interface
(total internal reflection at a glass--air exit face), where the
radicand in~\eqref{eq:snell} is clamped and the pattern acquires
$C^0$ kinks and large excursions.
\end{proposition}

The TIR set is not exotic: a random draw from the admissible box
crosses it during a 10-s scan with probability
\PH{$p_{\mathrm{TIR}}$, E1}, producing pattern jumps of hundreds of
spatial units over a handful
of samples.  An earlier version of this work asserted global
smoothness; the assertion is false as stated, and the inversion must
(and does, Sec.~\ref{sec:deglitch}) handle the TIR set explicitly.

\section{Structure Theory}
\label{sec:theory}
% =====================================================================

Throughout, $\vect{p}(t)=(x(t),y(t))$ is the workpiece trace sampled
uniformly at $f_s=1/\Delta t$ over $[0,T)$, and
\begin{equation}
  z(t) = x(t) + i\,y(t)
\end{equation}
is its analytic combination.  Working with $z$ rather than $x,y$
separately is essential: a complex signal has no conjugate symmetry, so
positive and negative frequencies are distinct observables.

\subsection{The frequency lattice}

\begin{theorem}[Lattice support]
\label{thm:lattice}
Fix a parameter vector $\vect{\theta}$ and let
$\Phi:\mathbb{T}^{P}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}$ be the workpiece map evaluated
at rotor angles $\vect{\gamma}\in\mathbb{T}^{P}$, so that
$\vect{p}(t)=\Phi\bigl(2\pi N_1t+\alpha_{y,1},\dots,
2\pi N_Pt+\alpha_{y,P}\bigr)$.  Assume
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(H1)] at every $\vect{\gamma}\in\mathbb{T}^{P}$ and every
  interface the Snell radicand in~\eqref{eq:snell} is
  $\ge\rho_0>0$ (no TIR anywhere on the rotor torus);
\item[(H2)] every ray--face intersection on $\mathbb{T}^{P}$ is
  transversal: the intersection denominators are $\ge\rho_1>0$.
\end{enumerate}
Then there exist $C<\infty$ and $\eta>0$, depending only on
$(\rho_0,\rho_1)$ and the parameter box, such that
\begin{equation}
  z(t) \;=\; \sum_{\vect{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^P} c_{\vect{k}}\,
  e^{2\pi i\,(\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N})\,t},
  \qquad
  |c_{\vect{k}}|\;\le\;C\,e^{-\eta\,|\vect{k}|_1},
  \label{eq:latticemodel}
\end{equation}
with the series absolutely and uniformly convergent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each interface normal~\eqref{eq:normal} depends on $\gamma_i$ only
through $\cos\gamma_i$ and $\sin\gamma_i$, which are restrictions to
the unit polytorus of the functions $(u_i+u_i^{-1})/2$ and
$(u_i-u_i^{-1})/2i$, holomorphic on $(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{P}$ in
$u_i=e^{i\gamma_i}$.  The trace is obtained from the normals by a
\emph{finite} composition of field operations, square roots
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:snell}], and two-line intersections (rational).
Extend this composition step by step to the polyannulus
$A_{r}=\{r^{-1}\le|u_i|\le r\}^{P}$: at each step, the functions
already constructed are holomorphic on $A_{r}$ and continuous up to
the boundary for $r$ close enough to $1$; by (H1) each radicand is
$\ge\rho_0$ on the compact torus, so by continuity and compactness it
stays in the slit plane $\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]$ on $A_{r}$
for $r$ close to $1$, where the principal square root is holomorphic;
by (H2) each denominator stays nonzero on $A_{r}$ likewise.  Since
the composition has finitely many steps ($2P$ interfaces), a single
$r>1$ works for all of them, and $\Phi_1+i\Phi_2$ extends
holomorphically to $A_{r}$.  For any $\vect{k}$, apply Cauchy's
formula on the polycircle $|u_i|=r^{\operatorname{sign}k_i}$
(radius $1$ where $k_i=0$):
\begin{align*}
  |c_{\vect{k}}|
  &=\Bigl|\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{P}}\oint\!\cdots\!\oint
  \frac{(\Phi_1+i\Phi_2)(\vect{u})\,d\vect{u}}
        {u_1^{k_1+1}\cdots u_P^{k_P+1}}\Bigr|\\
  &\le\;\|\Phi_1+i\Phi_2\|_{A_r}\;r^{-|\vect{k}|_1},
\end{align*}
which is the stated decay with $\eta=\log r$ and
$C=\|\Phi_1+i\Phi_2\|_{A_r}<\infty$ by compactness.  Absolute uniform
convergence of the multiple Fourier series follows by summing the
geometric bound.  Evaluating along the orbit
$\gamma_i(t)=2\pi N_it+\alpha_{y,i}$ turns the monomial
$\vect{u}^{\vect{k}}$ into
$e^{i\vect{k}\cdot\vect{\alpha}_y}\,
e^{2\pi i(\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N})t}$; absorbing the constant phase
into $c_{\vect{k}}$ gives~\eqref{eq:latticemodel}.
\end{proof}

Configurations violating (H1) on part of the torus are exactly the
TIR draws of Prop.~\ref{prop:tir}: there the expansion holds on the
sub-orbit where the radicands stay positive, and the algorithm masks
the complement (Sec.~\ref{sec:deglitch}).  \emph{Test A4} measures
the practical truncation: projecting the exact trace onto
$|\vect{k}|_1\le4$ leaves median relative residual \PH{A4} over the
campaign ensemble, exceeding $10^{-2}$ essentially only on TIR draws
(fraction \PH{A4b}).

Three refinements of Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} carry the inversion.

\begin{lemma}[Paraxial cascade gains]
\label{lem:gains}
Linearize the per-axis forward map at zero wedge angles
(all $\alpha_{x,j}=0$), with glass, geometry, and beam fixed.  Let
$K_{x,i}$ [resp.\ $K_{y,i}$] denote the derivative of the workpiece
$x$ [resp.\ $y$] coordinate with respect to an $x$-slope [resp.\
$y$-slope] perturbation injected at the exit face of prism $i$.  Then
\begin{align}
  K_{\bullet,i}
  &=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_i}\ell_{s}\sec^{2}\!\theta_{s}
  \prod_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{i,s}}D_{m},
  \label{eq:gains}\\
  D_{m}&=\frac{n_{m^{-}}\cos\theta_{m^{-}}}
             {n_{m^{+}}\cos\theta_{m^{+}}}\;>\;0,
  \nonumber
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{S}_i$ is the set of ray segments downstream of prism
$i$, $\mathcal{M}_{i,s}$ the set of interfaces crossed between prism
$i$ and segment $s$, and $\theta$ the unperturbed per-axis ray
angles.  In particular $K_{x,i},K_{y,i}>0$; they differ only through
the $\theta$-dependence induced by the beam angles.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At zero wedge every face is perpendicular to $z$, so the per-axis
Snell law at interface $m$ is
$n_{m^-}\sin\theta_{m^-}=n_{m^+}\sin\theta_{m^+}$; away from TIR both
cosines are positive, and implicit differentiation gives
$d\theta_{m^+}/d\theta_{m^-}=D_{m}>0$.  A straight segment of length
$\ell_s$ maps $(x,\theta)\mapsto(x+\ell_s\tan\theta,\,\theta)$, with
$\partial x_{\mathrm{out}}/\partial\theta=\ell_s\sec^{2}\theta$ and
$\partial x_{\mathrm{out}}/\partial x=1$; a zero-wedge interface
leaves $x$ continuous.  The chain rule along the downstream
composition yields exactly~\eqref{eq:gains}: each summand is the
sensitivity path that propagates as an angle up to segment $s$ and as
a position thereafter, and every factor is strictly positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Signed fundamentals and the conjugate leak]
\label{prop:leak}
Write $\tau_i=\tan\alpha_{x,i}$ and
$\tilde\alpha_{y,i}=\alpha_{y,i}+180^\circ[\tau_i<0]$.  As
$\max_j|\tau_j|\to0$,
\begin{equation}
  z(t) = z_0+\sum_{i}\bigl(
        A_i\,e^{i\varphi_i(t)}
      + B_i\,e^{-i\varphi_i(t)}\bigr)+\bigO(\tau^{2}),
\end{equation}
with $\varphi_i(t)=2\pi N_i t+\tilde\alpha_{y,i}$,
$A_i=\tfrac12(K_{x,i}+K_{y,i})\,|\tau_i|$,
$B_i=\tfrac12(K_{x,i}-K_{y,i})\,|\tau_i|$, and the gains of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gains}.  Since both gains are strictly positive,
$|B_i|<A_i$ whenever $\tau_i\neq0$: the fundamental line at signed
frequency $N_i$ strictly dominates its conjugate at $-N_i$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the construction in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice}, $z$ is
jointly analytic in $(t,\vect{\tau})$ near $\vect{\tau}=\vect{0}$;
Taylor-expand to first order.  At $\vect{\tau}=\vect{0}$ all faces
are flat and the trace is the constant $z_0$ (the static ray).  The
first-order term in $\tau_i$ is computed from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal}: the transverse part of the exit-face normal is
$\tau_i(\cos\varphi_i,\sin\varphi_i)$ with
$\varphi_i=\gamma_i(t)+\alpha_{y,i}$, and differentiating the Snell
update at a flat interface shows the exit-slope perturbation is
$\lambda_i\tau_i(\cos\varphi_i,\sin\varphi_i)$ per axis with a common
factor $\lambda_i>0$ (the refraction contrast; positive away from
TIR).  Lemma~\ref{lem:gains} propagates this to the workpiece:
$\delta x=K_{x,i}\lambda_i\tau_i\cos\varphi_i$,
$\delta y=K_{y,i}\lambda_i\tau_i\sin\varphi_i$ (absorb $\lambda_i$
into the gains).  The identity
$K_x\cos\varphi+iK_y\sin\varphi
=\tfrac12(K_x{+}K_y)e^{i\varphi}+\tfrac12(K_x{-}K_y)e^{-i\varphi}$
together with $\tau e^{i\varphi}=|\tau|e^{i(\varphi+180^\circ[\tau<0])}$
gives the displayed form, and $K_x,K_y>0$ implies
$|K_x-K_y|<K_x+K_y$, i.e.\ $|B_i|<A_i$.
\end{proof}

The sign of every speed is therefore an observable of the complex
spectrum---no sign enumeration is ever needed---and the ratio
$|B_i/A_i|$ measures the $x$/$y$ gain asymmetry induced by the beam
angles.  \emph{Test A2}: median leak ratio \PH{A2}; the inequality
holds in all but a fraction \PH{A2b} of prism instances (the rare
violations, strongly non-paraxial cases, are caught by the verified
sign test of Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}).

\begin{proposition}[Phase equivariance and readout]
\label{prop:phase}
(i)~\emph{Exact:} for every $\vect{k}$,
$c_{\vect{k}}(\vect{\alpha}_y)
=c_{\vect{k}}(\vect{0})\,e^{i\vect{k}\cdot\vect{\alpha}_y}$; in
particular
$\arg c_{\vect{e}_i}=\alpha_{y,i}+\arg c_{\vect{e}_i}(\vect{0})$.
(ii)~\emph{Paraxial branch:}
$\arg c_{\vect{e}_i}(\vect{0})=180^\circ[\alpha_{x,i}<0]
+\bigO(\tau)$, hence
$\arg c_{\vect{e}_i}=\tilde\alpha_{y,i}+\bigO(\tau)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i)~$\alpha_{y,i}$ enters the forward map only through the sum
$\gamma_i+\alpha_{y,i}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:normal}], so the torus map of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} satisfies
$\Phi^{(\vect{\alpha}_y)}(\vect{\gamma})
=\Phi^{(\vect{0})}(\vect{\gamma}+\vect{\alpha}_y)$, and a translation
of the torus multiplies the $\vect{k}$-th Fourier coefficient by
$e^{i\vect{k}\cdot\vect{\alpha}_y}$.
(ii)~By Prop.~\ref{prop:leak} at $\vect{\alpha}_y=\vect{0}$,
$c_{\vect{e}_i}(\vect{0})=A_i\,e^{i\,180^\circ[\tau_i<0]}
+\bigO(\tau^{2})$ with $A_i>0$; since $|A_i|=\Theta(|\tau_i|)$, the
relative size of the remainder is $\bigO(\tau)$, and $\arg$ is
Lipschitz on $\{w:|w-A|\le\tfrac12 A\}$, giving the stated phase
error.
\end{proof}

\emph{Test A3}: median readout error \PH{A3} (90th percentile
\PH{A3b}) over the campaign ensemble.  The wedge orientations are read off the
spectrum; combined with the wide $\alpha_y$-basin of the completion
stage this eliminates any search over orientations.

\begin{proposition}[Amplitude law]
\label{prop:amp}
Fix all parameters except $\tau_i=\tan\alpha_{x,i}$ and write
$c(\tau_i)=c_{\vect{e}_i}$.  Under (H1)--(H2), $c$ is analytic and
odd on a neighborhood of the admissible tilt interval, and there are
constants $a_i>0$, $b_i$ (functions of the remaining parameters)
with
\begin{equation}
  |c(\tau_i)| \;=\; a_i|\tau_i| + b_i|\tau_i|^{3}
  + \bigO(|\tau_i|^{5}).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Prop.~\ref{prop:phase}(i), $|c|$ is independent of
$\alpha_{y,i}$, so take $\alpha_{y,i}=0$.  Analyticity in $\tau_i$ is
part of the holomorphic-extension argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} (the parameter enters the same finite
composition).  Oddness: the flip symmetry (Prop.~\ref{prop:flip})
gives $c_{\vect{e}_i}(-\tau_i;\,\alpha_{y,i}{=}180^\circ)
=c_{\vect{e}_i}(\tau_i;\,\alpha_{y,i}{=}0)$, while
equivariance gives
$c_{\vect{e}_i}(-\tau_i;\,180^\circ)
=-\,c_{\vect{e}_i}(-\tau_i;\,0)$; hence $c(-\tau)=-c(\tau)$.  An odd
analytic function factors as $c(\tau)=\tau\,g(\tau^{2})$ with $g$
analytic, and $g(0)=\tfrac12(K_{x,i}+K_{y,i})>0$ by
Prop.~\ref{prop:leak}.  Therefore
$|c(\tau)|=|\tau|\sqrt{G(\tau^{2})}$ with $G=|g|^{2}$ analytic and
$G(0)>0$, so $\sqrt{G}$ is analytic near $0$; expanding it to first
order in $\tau^{2}$ gives the display with $a_i=|g(0)|>0$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Test A6}: calibrating $(a_i,b_i)$ by two forward evaluations per
prism and inverting the cubic recovers $|\alpha_{x,i}|$ with median
error \PH{A6} when the calibration uses the true geometry and
\PH{A6b} (90th percentile \PH{A6c}) when it uses only nominal
mid-box geometry---inside the measured completion basin
(Sec.~\ref{sec:basin}) in either case.

\subsection{Why generator identification must be arithmetic}
\label{sec:finer}

The speeds are the \emph{generators} of the observed line set, and one
might hope to select them by fitting candidate generator tuples and
comparing residuals.  This fails structurally.

\begin{lemma}[Finer-lattice lemma]
\label{lem:finer}
Let $\mathcal{L}(\vect{g})=\{\vect{k}\cdot\vect{g}: |\vect{k}|_1\le B\}$
and let $\vect{g}'$ be any tuple with
$\mathcal{L}(\vect{g})\cap\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\vect{g}')$
on the observed line set $\mathcal{F}$.  Then the least-squares
residual of the $\vect{g}'$-lattice model is no larger than that of
$\vect{g}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\vect{g}'$ design spans a superset of the regressors on
$\mathcal{F}$; least squares is monotone under design enlargement.
\end{proof}

A compromise or subharmonic tuple whose lattice happens to cover the
strong lines (e.g.\ $g=1.096$ absorbing a line at $-3.289$ as
$\vect{k}=-3$ and one at $-1.846$ as $(1,-2)$, an actual development
failure of a residual-guided predecessor) therefore always ties or
beats the true generators on residual.  Selection must instead reward
\emph{parsimony}---amplitude mass concentrated at low lattice
order---which is an arithmetic property of the integer representation,
not a fit property.  This is the same reason integer-relation methods
(LLL~\cite{LLL1982}) exist: shortness of the representation, not
goodness of fit, identifies a basis.  Our coverage score
(Sec.~\ref{sec:selection}) implements exactly this.

\subsection{Identifiability}
\label{sec:identifiability}

Local identifiability at the completion stage is a numerical theorem:
the $400\times18$ Jacobian of the sampled forward map has full rank~18
at every tested solution, with condition number
$\kappa\approx5\times10^{5}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:svd}); by the inverse
function theorem the solution is locally unique.  The singular spectrum
spans roughly five orders of magnitude---from the speed directions
(largest $\sigma$) down to the $d_W\!\leftrightarrow\!\delta$
geometry trade-off (smallest $\sigma$)---which is why
first-order optimizers fail here
($\kappa(\vect{H})\approx\kappa^2\approx10^{10}$) while trust-region
Gauss--Newton succeeds, and why the certificate of
Sec.~\ref{sec:certify} must not truncate weak directions: the weakest
eigenvalue is genuine information, and it carries the dominant error
bound.  The classical speed condition $|N_i|\neq|N_j|$
remains necessary; its quantitative form is condition (i) below.

At the \emph{spectral} stage, identifiability is information-theoretic.
Let $\sigma(\hat N_i)$, $\sigma(\hat c_i)$ denote the Fisher standard
errors of the fitted lattice model (Sec.~\ref{sec:certify}).

\begin{definition}[Spectral identifiability]
\label{def:spectral}
A configuration is spectrally identifiable at observation time $T$ if
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{pair resolvability}:
  $\min_{i\ne j}\,\min(|N_i-N_j|,|N_i+N_j|) > 3\,\sigma_{ij}$,
\item[(ii)] \emph{prism detectability}:
  $|c_{\vect{e}_i}| > 5\,\sigma(\hat c_i)$ for all $i$,
\item[(iii)] \emph{lattice non-degeneracy}:
  $|\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}-N_j| > 3\sigma$ for all
  $|\vect{k}|_1\le4$, $\vect{k}\ne\pm\vect{e}_j$,
\item[(iv)] the TIR-masked sample fraction is below the mask budget,
\item[(v)] \emph{front-end capacity}: the number of lattice lines above
  the working floor does not exceed the line-model order of the
  spectral front end.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Conditions (i)--(iv) are properties of the \emph{signal}; (v) is a
property of the \emph{estimator}, and it is qualitatively different:
its violation is self-diagnosed at run time (the CLEAN residual sits
far above the lattice-adequacy floor of Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice})
and is curable \emph{without additional observation} by switching to a
capacity-free candidate source (Sec.~\ref{sec:clean}).  Deeply
non-paraxial systems reach this regime through a dense comb whose
tooth spacing is the minimal small-$\vect{k}$ lattice value; past
capacity a subspace line estimator returns cluster centroids rather
than lines, poisoning every downstream decision while the information
itself remains intact.

Conditions (i)--(iii) fail continuously, and each failure carries a
prescription for the observation time that would cure it,
$T_{\mathrm{req}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:treq}, derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:certify} from the $T^{-3/2}$ frequency-error law and the
estimator's resolution floor.  The campaign's target is
\emph{zero unexplained failures}: every recovery failure must be a
certified violation of (i)--(iv) (E1), and every prescription is
validated by bisection (E4).

\subsection{Wedge count and non-Risley targets}
\label{sec:fourier}

Two classical facts settle the wedge-count question.  The set
$\bigcup_P\mathcal{Q}_P$ of quasi-periodic functions is dense in
$L^2[0,T]$ (Stone--Weierstrass~\cite{StoneWeierstrass}), and for
targets $f\in W^{s,2}$ the best-$P$-prism residual obeys
$d(f,\mathcal{T}_P)^2\le C_s\|f\|^2_{W^{s,2}}\,P^{-(2s-1)}$ in the
paraxial limit~\cite{DeVore1993} (measured on a piecewise-linear
target: $d^2\sim P^{-1.64}$, MSE $20.2$ at $P{=}1$ down to $0.93$ at
$P{=}6$).  A true Risley pattern reaches $d=0$ exactly at the true
$P$: the prism count is identified as the $P$ whose residual is
numerical zero, and a strictly positive residual plateau is itself a
certificate that the target is not a Risley pattern of the assumed
geometry.

\section{The Inversion Algorithm}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

All stages operate on one sampled pattern ($T=10$\,s, $f_s=20$\,Hz,
200 samples in the reference acquisition).  The spectral stages perform \emph{no}
forward-model evaluations; the angle stage performs six; the completion
stage performs one bounded trust-region solve.  Median wall time is
$\sim$1\,s (spectral) plus 1--4\,s (completion) per instance.

\subsection{De-glitching the TIR set}
\label{sec:deglitch}

Samples adjacent to pattern jumps exceeding $10\times$ the median
step are masked (with a $15\%$ mask budget); the masked samples are
excluded from every subsequent fit, and linearly interpolated only for
the pencil initializer.  After the final lattice fit the mask is
recomputed self-consistently from the fit residual.  The TIR cases of
Prop.~\ref{prop:tir} are thereby handled, not excluded: the recovery
rate conditional on a TIR crossing is reported separately in the
atlas (\PH{E1: TIR class}).

\subsection{CLEAN line growth at order one}
\label{sec:clean}

The matrix pencil~\cite{HuaSarkar1990,RoyKailath1989} applied to the
(masked, de-meaned) signal returns super-resolved line frequencies and
complex amplitudes, free of the bin discretization that destroys
FFT-based phase readout.  Lines are accepted one at a time,
CLEAN-style~\cite{Hogbom1974}: take the strongest pencil line of the
current residual, add it to the line set, and refit \emph{all} line
frequencies jointly by variable projection
\cite{GolubPereyra1973} (amplitudes linear, frequencies by damped
Gauss--Newton) at lattice order $B{=}1$, where a line explains only
itself and Lemma~\ref{lem:finer} has no purchase.  Novel lines---those
not expressible as small-integer combinations of the current
set---take priority, so harmonics cannot exhaust the line budget
before a weak fundamental appears.  If the CLEAN residual reports
front-end overload [condition~(v)], the growth is repeated once with
capacity-free candidates---windowed-FFT peak maxima with parabolic
interpolation---whose precision the joint Gauss--Newton refit then
restores; the retry fires only on overloaded instances (development
testing found it curing the entire overload class at the original
observation time; the campaign measures the cure rate at scale,
\PH{E1: overload class}).

\subsection{Generator identification by integer coverage}
\label{sec:selection}

Candidate generator $P$-tuples among the fitted lines are ranked by
\emph{coverage}: the amplitude-weighted fraction of all lines
representable as $\vect{k}\cdot\vect{g}$ with $|\vect{k}|_1\le3$
(sampling aliases $f\pm f_s$ included), minus a small penalty on
representation order.  A consensus reweighting zeroes lines that no
leading candidate explains (CLEAN artifacts).  Per
Lemma~\ref{lem:finer}, coverage---not fit residual---is the primary
gate; the full lattice fit (order $B{=}3$, refined at $B{=}4$) referees
only among candidates within a small coverage band, and a candidate
whose generators drift far from the measured lines during refinement is
rejected outright.  Two deterministic repairs follow: a
large-amplitude line at order $\ge2$ is trial-promoted to a generator
(it may be a fundamental sitting on an accidental near-relation), and
if coverage reports unexplained lines, the strongest residual line is
trial-swapped for each generator.  Each repair is accepted only on a
large, guarded residual improvement.

Two numerical protections in the lattice fit are load-bearing.
Near-coincident lattice lines (closer than $0.012$\,Hz at $T=10$\,s)
are \emph{merged} onto their lowest-order representative---the data
cannot distinguish them, and an unmerged pair lets ridge regression
split large amplitudes across near-collinear columns; conversely the
amplitude solve is \emph{ridged}, because unregularized least squares
on near-collinear columns manufactures huge canceling amplitude pairs
that poison every amplitude-based decision.  A final
\textquotedblleft sharp\textquotedblright\ pass with both protections
off, from the converged basis, recovers the last decimal.

\subsection{Canonicalization and the sign test}
\label{sec:canon}

The physical fundamentals are extracted from the fitted model as the
rank-extending largest-amplitude lines (first order dominates:
\emph{Test A5} measures the violation rate at \PH{A5}, all
violations tolerated by the rank-extension rule); this single re-extraction can
rescue a mis-selected generator.  After the final refinement the
generators \emph{are} the speeds and are never re-extracted (from a
refit, re-extraction grabs ridge-splitting twins---a documented failure
of an earlier version).  Finally the sign of each speed is verified by
Prop.~\ref{prop:leak}: if the fitted amplitude at $-\vect{e}_i$
exceeds that at $+\vect{e}_i$, the sign is flipped and the model
refit.  The test runs \emph{unconditionally}---on every code path,
from a dedicated fit if necessary: an implementation that proves the
leak inequality but only sometimes enforces it will eventually return
an estimate violating it by an order of magnitude (an ensemble case
did exactly that before this was understood).

\subsection{Angle readout and completion}
\label{sec:angleread}

$\alpha_{y,i}$ is read from the fundamental phase
(Prop.~\ref{prop:phase}); the branch $|{\arg c}|>90^\circ$
simultaneously fixes $\operatorname{sign}\alpha_{x,i}$
(Prop.~\ref{prop:flip}).  $|\alpha_{x,i}|$ is obtained by inverting
the cubic amplitude law (Prop.~\ref{prop:amp}) with gains calibrated
by two forward evaluations per prism---against the known geometry in
the 9-parameter protocol, or against nominal mid-box geometry in the
full 18-parameter problem.  The beam angles are initialized
analytically from the pattern's DC component (first-order rotating
deflections average to zero, so the DC is the static ray); glass and
distances start mid-box.  A single bound-constrained trust-region
solve~\cite{Branch1999} on the exact model, with TIR-masked residuals,
completes the recovery to near machine precision.

If the pattern MSE does not reach the $10^{-12}$ acceptance
threshold, a deterministic \emph{verified ladder} is walked: gain
recalibration using the first fit's geometry, the phase-branch flips
of ambiguous prisms, a zero-angle start, a sign flip of the weakest
speed (the least reliable bit of the extraction, cf.\ the A2
violation), and the alternate generator tuples ranked by the spectral
stage.  Every rung is a fixed candidate accepted or rejected by the
final MSE---model selection, not search: the accepted solution
reproduces the data to $10^{-23}$--$10^{-26}$, twelve orders below any
rejected rung.

\subsection{Measured basin of the completion stage}
\label{sec:basin}

The staged design rests on one empirical claim: spectral-quality
initializations lie in the trust-region basin.  We measure it
directly (\emph{Test A7}): perturbing true solutions by draws matched
to the spectral stage's actual error scale (speeds
$3\times10^{-4}$\,Hz, $\alpha_x$ $1.5^\circ$, $\alpha_y$ $2^\circ$,
glass $0.08$, $d_W$ $40$, gap $4$, beam $2$--$3^\circ$), the 18-D
trust-region solve converges in \PH{A7} of trials; at $4\times$ that
error scale, still \PH{A7b}.  The historical
\textquotedblleft narrow basin\textquotedblright\ of this problem was
a property of uninformed initializations, not of the landscape.

\section{Error Analysis and Certificates}
\label{sec:certify}
% =====================================================================

This section derives the error bounds; nothing in it is asserted from
simulation.  The logical structure is: a completion-stage bound that is
valid \emph{whenever the residual test accepts, regardless of how the
initializer arrived there} (Prop.~\ref{prop:decomp}); and a
spectral-stage information analysis that governs \emph{whether} the
pipeline arrives, with closed-form scaling laws
(Props.~\ref{prop:crb}--\ref{prop:detect}).  The campaign of
Sec.~\ref{sec:campaign} then \emph{validates} the derived bounds rather
than substituting for them.

\subsection{Completion stage: the certified bound}

Let $F:\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{18}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}$ be the sampled
(masked) forward map, $\vect{y}=F(\vect{\theta}^{*})+\vect{\varepsilon}$
the observation, $\hat{\vect{\theta}}$ the accepted iterate,
$\vect{J}=DF(\hat{\vect{\theta}})$ (full column rank by
Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}), and
$\vect{r}=F(\hat{\vect{\theta}})-\vect{y}$ the residual.

\begin{proposition}[Error decomposition]
\label{prop:decomp}
If $F$ is $C^{2}$ on the segment
$[\hat{\vect{\theta}},\vect{\theta}^{*}]$ with
$L_{2}=\sup\lVert D^{2}F\rVert$ there, then
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\vect{\theta}}-\vect{\theta}^{*}
  = \underbrace{(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}
    \vect{r}}_{\text{optimality gap (computable)}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}
    \vect{\varepsilon}}_{\text{noise term}}
  \;+\;\vect{\rho},
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
with $\lVert\vect{\rho}\rVert\le\tfrac12 L_{2}
\lVert(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}\rVert\,
\lVert\hat{\vect{\theta}}-\vect{\theta}^{*}\rVert^{2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taylor with integral remainder gives
$F(\vect{\theta}^{*})=F(\hat{\vect{\theta}})
+\vect{J}(\vect{\theta}^{*}-\hat{\vect{\theta}})+\vect{R}$,
$\lVert\vect{R}\rVert\le\tfrac12 L_{2}
\lVert\vect{\theta}^{*}-\hat{\vect{\theta}}\rVert^{2}$.  Substituting
into $\vect{r}=F(\hat{\vect{\theta}})-F(\vect{\theta}^{*})
-\vect{\varepsilon}$ and applying
$(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}$ rearranges
to~\eqref{eq:decomp} with
$\vect{\rho}=(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}
\vect{R}$.
\end{proof}

The first term of~\eqref{eq:decomp} is exactly the remaining
Gauss--Newton step: the certificate's gap term is an algebraic identity,
not a heuristic---it vanishes at a stationary point and exactly accounts
for early stopping.  The second term is mean-zero with covariance
$\sigma^{2}(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}$ under white noise of
variance $\sigma^{2}$, and $s^{2}
=\lVert\vect{r}\rVert^{2}/(m-18)$ is its standard unbiased surrogate in
the locally linear regime.

\begin{corollary}[Certificate]
\label{cor:cert}
Under $\vect{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\vect{I})$ and
local linearity ($\vect{\rho}$ subdominant),
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bounds}
  b_i \;=\; 3\big[s^2 (\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\big]_{ii}^{1/2}
  \;+\; \big|(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}\vect{J}^{\top}
  \vect{r}\big|_i
\end{equation}
covers $|\hat\theta_i-\theta_i^{*}|$ with probability $\ge 0.997$ per
parameter.  In the noiseless numerical setting $\vect{\varepsilon}$ is
the arithmetic-plus-truncation floor, the gap term dominates, and the
bound is deterministic up to $\vect{\rho}$.
\end{corollary}

Three remarks, each with teeth.  (i)~\emph{No covariance truncation is
admissible}: removing the smallest eigenpair of
$\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J}$ deletes precisely its largest contribution to
$[(\vect{J}^{\top}\vect{J})^{-1}]_{ii}$, which lies along the
$d_W\!\leftrightarrow\!\delta$ direction whose eigenvalue falls to
$\sim\!10^{-15}$ of the largest on the worst-conditioned ensemble
instances---far below the typical $\sim\!10^{-12}$ set by
$\kappa\approx5\times10^{5}$; rank~18
(Sec.~\ref{sec:identifiability}) guarantees invertibility, and a
conventional $10^{-14}$ pseudo-inverse cutoff measurably broke coverage
on one development case before this was understood.
(ii)~\emph{Path independence}: nothing in
Prop.~\ref{prop:decomp} references the initializer.  Once the verified
ladder accepts, the certificate holds even if every spectral heuristic
had been wrong---the spectral stage controls only whether acceptance
occurs.  (iii)~\emph{Calibration is testable}: at $3\sigma$ the
per-parameter miss rate should be $\approx0.3\%$; the campaign's E2
experiment tests this against $\sim\!10^{7}$ ground-truth checks
(measured miss rate \PH{E2}), resolving mis-calibration at the
$10^{-4}$ level---the bounds must come out statistically calibrated,
neither optimistic nor padded.

\subsection{Spectral stage: information limits}

Model the analytic signal as
$z_j=\sum_{\vect{k}\in K}c_{\vect{k}}
e^{2\pi i(\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N})t_j}+w_j$ at $t_j=j/f_s$,
$j=0,\dots,M{-}1$, $T=M/f_s$, with complex white noise of variance
$\sigma_w^{2}$ (in noiseless data, the lattice-truncation floor of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} plays this role).

\begin{lemma}[Uniform-grid kernel bound]
\label{lem:kernel}
For $t_j=j/f_s$, $j=0,\dots,M{-}1$, an offset $0<|\delta|\le f_s/2$,
and any weight sequence $q(t_j)$ that is monotone in $j$ with range
in $[0,1]$ (e.g.\ $q=(t/T)^{m}$, $m\le2$),
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl|\sum_{j} q(t_j)\,e^{2\pi i\delta t_j}\Bigr|
  \;\le\;\frac{f_s}{|\delta|}
  \;=\;\frac{M}{|\delta|\,T}.
  \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $q\equiv1$ the sum is geometric with modulus
$|\sin(\pi\delta M/f_s)|/|\sin(\pi\delta/f_s)|
\le1/\sin(\pi|\delta|/f_s)\le f_s/(2|\delta|)$, using
$\sin\pi x\ge2x$ on $[0,\tfrac12]$.  For general monotone $q$ with
range in $[0,1]$, Abel summation against the partial sums $S_J$
(each $|S_J|\le f_s/2|\delta|$) gives the bound
$(\sup|q|+\mathrm{TV}(q))\max_J|S_J|\le2\cdot f_s/(2|\delta|)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Lattice Fisher information]
\label{prop:crb}
The Fisher matrix of
$(\vect{N},\operatorname{Re}\vect{c},\operatorname{Im}\vect{c})$
under the complex-Gaussian model is \emph{exactly}
$(2/\sigma_w^{2})\operatorname{Re}(\vect{X}^{H}\vect{X})$ with
$\vect{X}=[\vect{J}_{N},\vect{E},i\vect{E}]$, where $\vect{E}$ is the
Vandermonde design and $\vect{J}_{N}$ its derivative in the
generators---the matrix the failure certificate inverts.  Let
$\Delta$ be the minimal separation of distinct occupied (folded)
lattice frequencies, $w_{\max}$ the ratio of largest to smallest
fitted line weight, and suppose
$\varepsilon:=C_0\,n_{\mathrm{lines}}\,w_{\max}/(\Delta T)\le\tfrac12$
for an absolute constant $C_0$.  Then, with the generator Gram
matrix $G_{il}=\sum_{\vect{k}}k_ik_l|c_{\vect{k}}|^{2}$,
\begin{align}
  \sigma(\hat N_i)^{2}
  &=\frac{3\,\sigma_w^{2}}{2\pi^{2} f_s\,T^{3}}\,
  [G^{-1}]_{ii}\,
  \bigl(1+\bigO(\varepsilon)+\bigO(M^{-2})\bigr)
  \label{eq:crb}\\
  &\ge\frac{3\,\sigma_w^{2}}{2\pi^{2} f_s\,T^{3}\,S_i}\,
  \bigl(1-\bigO(\varepsilon)\bigr),
  \nonumber
\end{align}
where $S_i=G_{ii}=\sum_{\vect{k}}k_i^{2}|c_{\vect{k}}|^{2}$,
with equality in the second line iff $G$ is diagonal (decoupled
generators); the scaling laws below use the conservative diagonal
evaluation, while the computed certificate always inverts the full
matrix.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Exact form.}  For $z_j=\mu_j+w_j$ with circular complex
Gaussian noise the log-likelihood is
$-\sigma_w^{-2}\sum_j|z_j-\mu_j|^{2}+\mathrm{const}$, whence
$I_{ab}=(2/\sigma_w^{2})\operatorname{Re}\sum_j
\overline{\partial_a\mu_j}\,\partial_b\mu_j$; with
$\partial\mu_j/\partial N_i
=2\pi i\,t_j\sum_{\vect{k}}k_ic_{\vect{k}}
e^{2\pi i\omega_{\vect{k}}t_j}$ and amplitude columns
$\vect{E},i\vect{E}$ this is the stated matrix.

\emph{Block structure.}  Rescale the generators to
$\nu_i=2\pi N_iT$ so that every design column has entries bounded by
its line weight times $(t_j/T)^{m}$, $m\le1$.  Same-line inner
products are exact moment sums
$\sum_j(t_j/T)^{m+m'}=M/(m{+}m'{+}1)+\bigO(1)$; cross-line inner
products carry $e^{2\pi i\delta t_j}$ with $|\delta|\ge\Delta$ and
are bounded by $M/(\Delta T)$ times the two line weights
(Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel}).  Hence
$I=(2M/\sigma_w^{2})(\vect{D}+\vect{R})$ with $\vect{D}$
block-diagonal per line and
$\|\vect{D}^{-1}\vect{R}\|\le\varepsilon$; the Neumann series gives
$I^{-1}=(\sigma_w^{2}/2M)\vect{D}^{-1}
\bigl(1+\bigO(\varepsilon)\bigr)$.

\emph{Schur complement.}  Within the block of line $\vect{k}$,
eliminating the two amplitude coordinates replaces the raw second
moment $\sum_j(t_j/T)^{2}$ by the centered one,
$\sum_j(t_j-\bar t)^{2}/T^{2}=\tfrac{M}{12}(1-M^{-2})$ exactly
[$t_j$ is a uniform grid]; the factor multiplying it in the
$(N_i,N_l)$ entry is $(2\pi T)^{2}k_ik_l|c_{\vect{k}}|^{2}$.
Summing over lines, the generator block of $I$ after elimination is
$(2/\sigma_w^{2})(2\pi T)^{2}\tfrac{M}{12}\,G\,
(1+\bigO(\varepsilon)+\bigO(M^{-2}))$; inverting and using $M=f_sT$
gives~\eqref{eq:crb}.  The final inequality is
$[G^{-1}]_{ii}\ge1/G_{ii}$, valid for every positive-definite $G$:
$1=(\vect{e}_i^{\top}G^{1/2}G^{-1/2}\vect{e}_i)^{2}
\le(\vect{e}_i^{\top}G\vect{e}_i)
(\vect{e}_i^{\top}G^{-1}\vect{e}_i)$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.  The
single-line special case recovers the classical single-tone bound
\cite{RifeBoorstyn1974}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Observation-time law]
\label{cor:tscale}
At fixed sampling rate, $\sigma(\hat N_i)\propto T^{-3/2}$; a
separation margin deficient by the factor $3\sigma/\Delta$ is closed at
$T_{\mathrm{stat}}=T\,(3\sigma/\Delta)^{2/3}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Pair degradation and the resolution floor]
\label{prop:pair}
(i)~For two unit-weight lines at gap $g$ with $g/f_s\le\tfrac12$, the
normalized column inner product has modulus
$|\rho(g)|=\sin(\pi gT)\,/\,\bigl(M\sin(\pi g/f_s)\bigr)$ exactly,
and for $gT\le1$,
\begin{equation*}
  |\rho|^{2}=1-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}(gT)^{2}\bigl(1-M^{-2}\bigr)
  +\bigO\!\bigl((gT)^{4}\bigr);
\end{equation*}
consequently the $2\times2$ amplitude Gram matrix
$M\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\rho\\\bar\rho&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
has smallest eigenvalue $M(1-|\rho|)=\Theta(M(gT)^{2})$, and the
least-squares variance of the amplitude-difference coordinate
inflates by the factor
$(1-|\rho|^{2})^{-1}=\Theta\bigl((gT)^{-2}\bigr)$ relative to the
separated case.
(ii)~Independently, the estimator merges lines closer than
$C_{\mathrm{m}}/T$ ($C_{\mathrm{m}}=0.12$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:selection})---a deliberate trade of super-resolution
for robustness of the amplitude solve---so a pair or lattice-relation
gap $g$ is resolvable only if $T\ge c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}/g$, and the
certified prescription is
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{req}}
  \;=\;\max\Bigl(\,T\,(3\sigma/g)^{2/3},\;
  c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}/g\,\Bigr),\qquad c\approx2.
  \label{eq:treq}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i)~The inner product of the columns $(e^{2\pi ift_j})_j$ and
$(e^{2\pi i(f+g)t_j})_j$ is the geometric sum
$\sum_je^{2\pi igt_j}
=e^{i\pi g(M-1)/f_s}\,\sin(\pi gM/f_s)/\sin(\pi g/f_s)$; dividing by
$M$ and using $T=M/f_s$ gives $|\rho|$.  Apply
$\sin x=x(1-x^{2}/6+\bigO(x^{4}))$ to numerator
($x=\pi gT$) and denominator ($x=\pi g/f_s=\pi gT/M$) and square.
The eigenvalues of the Hermitian Gram form are $M(1\pm|\rho|)$, and
$1-|\rho|=\tfrac12(1-|\rho|^{2})(1+\bigO((gT)^{2}))$.  Explicit
$2\times2$ inversion of the normal equations shows the variance of
the difference coordinate carries the factor $(1-|\rho|^{2})^{-1}$.
(ii)~is the definition of the merge step; the safety constant $c$ is
measured, not derived (Sec.~\ref{sec:empirical}).
\end{proof}

The second argument of~\eqref{eq:treq} was forced on us by
development-scale testing: in an ensemble of several hundred
configurations, every unsolved
lattice-relation case had $g=1.4$--$3.2$\,mHz, satisfying the
statistical term but violating the merge floor
($cC_{\mathrm{m}}/g=75$--$170$\,s, beyond the $80$\,s ladder of that
experiment)---the corrected formula classifies all of them.  E4
re-tests the corrected prescription at campaign scale.

\begin{proposition}[Detectability]
\label{prop:detect}
If all pairwise separations of occupied lines are $\ge\Delta$ and
$n_{\mathrm{lines}}/(\Delta T)\le\tfrac12$, then
$\operatorname{var}(\hat c_{\vect{k}})
=(\sigma_w^{2}/M)\,\bigl(1+\bigO(n_{\mathrm{lines}}/(\Delta T))\bigr)$,
so the $5\sigma$ detection test requires
$|c_{\vect{e}_i}|\gtrsim 5\sigma_w/\sqrt{f_sT}\propto T^{-1/2}$;
through the amplitude law (Prop.~\ref{prop:amp}) the minimum
detectable wedge magnitude is
$\alpha_{\min}=\arctan\!\bigl(5\,\sigma(\hat c)/a_i\bigr)$, which is
the figure the weak-prism certificate reports.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The amplitude Gram matrix is $M(\vect{I}+\vect{R})$ with
$|R_{\vect{k}\vect{k}'}|\le1/(\Delta T)$ for $\vect{k}\ne\vect{k}'$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel}), so
$\|\vect{R}\|\le n_{\mathrm{lines}}/(\Delta T)\le\tfrac12$ and the
least-squares covariance is
$\sigma_w^{2}[(\vect{X}^{H}\vect{X})^{-1}]_{\vect{k}\vect{k}}
=(\sigma_w^{2}/M)(1+\bigO(\|\vect{R}\|))$ by the Neumann series; then
$M=f_sT$, the $5\sigma$ rule, and inversion of the (odd,
monotone-near-zero) amplitude law give the threshold.
\end{proof}

\subsection{What is derived, what is measured}
\label{sec:empirical}

The bound~\eqref{eq:bounds} and the laws
\eqref{eq:crb}--\eqref{eq:treq} are analysis; two links in the chain
are irreducibly empirical and are measured rather than assumed: the
basin of the completion stage (Test~A7---no convexity theory is
available for this landscape, so we measure the basin at the actual
init-error scale), and the constant $c$ in the merge floor.  Every
other constant in the certificates is computed from the data instance
at hand.

\section{Scaling Theory for Arbitrary Prism Count}
\label{sec:scaling}
% =====================================================================

Everything so far holds for any $P$: Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} and
Props.~\ref{prop:leak}--\ref{prop:amp} are per-prism statements, the
algorithm of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} takes the generator count as a
parameter, and the certificates of Sec.~\ref{sec:certify} are
dimension-agnostic.  What changes with $P$ is the \emph{information
geometry}: the unknown vector grows linearly,
$\dim\vect{\theta}=4P+6$ (four per-prism parameters---speed, two
wedge components, index---plus six global ones), while the spectral
population it must be read from grows polynomially and its margins
shrink.  This section makes those statements theorems.  Throughout,
the speed magnitudes $m_i=|N_i|$ are i.i.d.\ uniform on $[\nu,W]$
(the admissible box), $L=W-\nu$, signs independent, and the working
lattice orders are fixed integers: $B$ for fitting and $K$ for
relation screening (the implementation uses $B{=}3$, $K{=}4$).

\subsection{Cost is polynomial in $P$}

\begin{lemma}[Lattice population]
\label{lem:count}
Let $\mathcal{K}(P,B)=\{\vect{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{P}:0<|\vect{k}|_{1}\le
B\}$.  Then, for fixed $B$,
\begin{equation}
  |\mathcal{K}(P,B)| \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{\min(P,B)} 2^{j}
  \binom{P}{j}\binom{B}{j}
  \;=\;\Theta\!\bigl(P^{B}\bigr).
  \label{eq:count}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A vector with exactly $j$ nonzero entries is determined by its support
($\binom{P}{j}$ choices), the signs of those entries ($2^{j}$), and
the magnitudes, i.e.\ an integer composition
$m_{1}+\dots+m_{j}\le B$ with $m_{i}\ge1$, of which there are
$\binom{B}{j}$.  Summing over $j$ gives the identity; for $P>B$ the
$j=B$ term dominates, giving
$2^{B}\binom{P}{B}=\Theta(P^{B})$.
\end{proof}

Hence the lattice design matrices have $\Theta(P^{B})$ columns, the
completion Jacobian is $2M\times(4P+6)$, and every stage costs
polynomial time in $P$ (tuple selection ranks candidates by amplitude
and coverage; it never enumerates exhaustively).  The \emph{readout}
is strictly linear in $P$: orientations are $P$ line phases,
magnitudes invert $P$ scalar cubics calibrated by $2P$ forward
evaluations.  Nothing in the algorithm is exponential in $P$; the
obstructions below are informational, not computational.

\subsection{Crowding: pair resolvability costs $\Theta(P^{2})$}

\begin{proposition}[Exact crowding law]
\label{prop:crowd}
Let $g_{\mathrm{pair}}=\min_{i\ne j}\min(|N_i-N_j|,|N_i+N_j|)$.  For
$s<2\nu$,
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\bigl[g_{\mathrm{pair}}>s\bigr]
  =\Bigl(1-\frac{(P-1)\,s}{L}\Bigr)_{+}^{P},
  \quad
  \mathbb{E}\,g_{\mathrm{pair}}=\frac{L}{P^{2}-1}.
  \label{eq:crowd}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Whichever branch of $\min(|N_i-N_j|,|N_i+N_j|)$ is not
$|m_i-m_j|$ equals $m_i+m_j\ge2\nu$, so for $s<2\nu$ the pair gap is
the minimum spacing of the $P$ i.i.d.\ magnitudes on $[\nu,W]$.
Conditioning on the order statistics, the $P{+}1$ spacings (two edge
gaps, $P{-}1$ interior gaps) are uniform on the simplex
$\{d_j\ge0,\ \sum d_j=L\}$.  Subtracting $s$ from each interior gap
maps the event $\{$all interior gaps $>s\}$ affinely onto the same
simplex with total length $L-(P-1)s$; uniform measure on a
$P$-dimensional simplex scales as the $P$-th power of its linear
size, giving the survival function.  Integrating it,
$\int_{0}^{L/(P-1)}\!\bigl(1-\tfrac{(P-1)s}{L}\bigr)^{P}ds
=\tfrac{L}{(P-1)(P+1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Crowding-limited observation time]
\label{cor:crowdT}
By the merge floor (Prop.~\ref{prop:pair}), resolving all pairs
requires $T\ge c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}/g_{\mathrm{pair}}$; by
\eqref{eq:crowd},
$\Pr[g_{\mathrm{pair}}<s]\le P(P-1)s/L$, so condition (i) holds with
probability $\ge1-\delta$ once
\begin{equation}
  T \;\ge\; T_{\mathrm{pair}}(P,\delta)
  \;=\;\frac{c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}\,P(P-1)}{L\,\delta}
  \;=\;\Theta\!\Bigl(\frac{P^{2}}{\delta}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:crowdT}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Accidental relations: a degree-$K$ demand}

Condition (iii) requires the fundamentals to avoid the rest of the
lattice.  Writing coincidences $\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}\approx N_j$ as
relations $(\vect{k}-\vect{e}_j)\cdot\vect{N}\approx0$, the governing
quantity is
$g_{\mathrm{rel}}=\min\{|\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}|:
\vect{k}\in\mathcal{K}(P,K),\ \vect{k}\ne\pm q\vect{e}_{j}\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Relation-gap anti-concentration]
\label{prop:rel}
For every $\varepsilon<\nu$,
\begin{equation}
  \Pr\bigl[g_{\mathrm{rel}}<\varepsilon\bigr]
  \;\le\;\frac{2\,\varepsilon}{L}\,\bigl|\mathcal{K}(P,K)\bigr|
  \;=\;\Theta\!\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon\,P^{K}}{L}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:relbound}
\end{equation}
Moreover the expected number of relations below $\varepsilon$ is also
$\Theta(\varepsilon P^{K}/L)$, which is $\Theta(1)$ at
$\varepsilon\sim L P^{-K}$: no better polynomial rate is available
from first moments, and the campaign measures the empirical law of
$g_{\mathrm{rel}}$ directly (\PH{E1: margin histograms}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\vect{k}$ with at least two nonzero entries and let $i^{*}$
index a largest-magnitude entry, $1\le|k_{i^{*}}|\le K$.
Conditioning on all coordinates except $m_{i^{*}}$ (signs are fixed
within a configuration), the map
$m_{i^{*}}\mapsto\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}$ is affine with slope of
modulus $|k_{i^{*}}|\ge1$, so the conditional density of
$\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}$ is bounded by $1/(|k_{i^{*}}|L)\le1/L$ and
$\Pr[|\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}|<\varepsilon]\le2\varepsilon/L$.
Single-support vectors $\pm q\vect{e}_j$ are excluded, and for them
$|q\,m_j|\ge\nu>\varepsilon$ anyway.  The union bound over
$\mathcal{K}(P,K)$ with Lemma~\ref{lem:count}
gives~\eqref{eq:relbound}; linearity of expectation over the same
family, with each term also bounded \emph{below} by
$c'\varepsilon/L$ for the $\Theta(P^{K})$ vectors whose value range
straddles zero, gives the expected count.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Relation-limited observation time]
\label{cor:relT}
Setting $\varepsilon=c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}/T$ in~\eqref{eq:relbound},
condition (iii) holds with probability $\ge1-\delta$ once
\begin{equation}
  T \;\ge\; T_{\mathrm{rel}}(P,\delta)
  \;=\;\Theta\!\Bigl(\frac{C_{\mathrm{m}}\,P^{K}}{L\,\delta}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:relT}
\end{equation}
polynomial of degree $K$---the dominant demand once $K>2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Pigeonhole at unbounded order]
\label{prop:pigeon}
For any $\vect{N}\in[-W,W]^{P}$ and any integer $Q\ge1$ there exists
$\vect{k}\ne\vect{0}$, $|\vect{k}|_{\infty}\le Q$, with
\begin{equation}
  |\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}| \;\le\; \frac{P\,Q\,W}{(Q+1)^{P}-1}.
  \label{eq:pigeon}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $(Q+1)^{P}$ values $\{\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}:\vect{k}\in
\{0,\dots,Q\}^{P}\}$ lie in an interval of length $PQW$; two are
within $PQW/((Q+1)^{P}-1)$ of each other, and their difference is the
required vector.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pigeon} is exponentially small in $P$: if the
working order ever had to \emph{grow} with $P$, near-relations would
become unavoidable and identifiability would collapse.  The saving
fact is that it does not: the geometric coefficient decay of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lattice} confines the observable spectrum to bounded
order, so the demand stays polynomial~\eqref{eq:relT}.  This is the
precise sense in which the large-$P$ problem is Diophantine, and it
identifies the bounded-order window as the load-bearing structural
assumption.

\subsection{Capacity and detectability}

\begin{proposition}[Capacity threshold]
\label{prop:capscale}
For every $\vect{N}$, some pair of the folded lattice frequencies
$\{\vect{k}\cdot\vect{N}\bmod f_{s}:\vect{k}\in\mathcal{K}(P,B)\}$
lies within $f_{s}/|\mathcal{K}(P,B)|$ of each other.  Hence once
$T\gtrsim(C_{\mathrm{m}}/f_{s})\,|\mathcal{K}(P,B)|
=(C_{\mathrm{m}}/f_{s})\,\Theta(P^{B})$, some lattice spacing lies
below the merge floor for \emph{every} configuration: the dense-comb
regime of condition (v) is generic rather than exceptional, and the
front end must run in its capacity-free mode (Sec.~\ref{sec:clean}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Pigeonhole: $|\mathcal{K}(P,B)|$ points on a circle of circumference
$f_{s}$ contain a pair at arc distance
$\le f_{s}/|\mathcal{K}(P,B)|$; compare with $C_{\mathrm{m}}/T$ and
apply Lemma~\ref{lem:count}.
\end{proof}
The campaign locates the threshold empirically by the overload-retry
rate as a function of $P$ (\PH{E5}).

\begin{proposition}[Detectability decays at most geometrically in $P$]
\label{prop:detscale}
Let $D_{\min}\le D_m\le D_{\max}$ bound the per-interface transfer
derivatives of Lemma~\ref{lem:gains} over the admissible box, with
$D_{\min}\ge(n_{\min}/n_{\max})\cos\theta_{\max}>0$.  Then the
paraxial gain of prism $i$ obeys
\begin{align}
  K_{\bullet,i}&\;\ge\;d_{W}\,D_{\min}^{\,2(P-i)},
  \label{eq:gainbounds}\\
  K_{\bullet,i}&\;\le\;
  \bigl(d_{W}+(P{-}i)(\tau_{\mathrm{p}}{+}\delta)\bigr)
  \sec^{2}\!\theta_{\max}\,D_{\max}^{\,2(P-i)},
  \nonumber
\end{align}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$ is the prism thickness.  Hence $\log a_i$
[Prop.~\ref{prop:amp}] varies at most linearly in $P$ with slope
bounded by $2\log D_{\max}$; at zero beam angles every $\theta=0$,
each prism traversal contributes
$D=(1/n_{\mathrm{g}})\cdot n_{\mathrm{g}}=1$ exactly, and the gains
are $P$-independent up to the linear growth of the optical path.
Condition (ii) therefore degrades at most geometrically in $P$ with
an explicitly bounded rate, while the lattice population grows
polynomially (Lemma~\ref{lem:count}): for moderate $P$ the binding
constraints are crowding and relations, not detectability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the lower bound keep only the final summand
of~\eqref{eq:gains}---the workpiece segment of length $d_W$, reached
after the $2(P-i)$ downstream interfaces---and bound each transfer
derivative below by $D_{\min}$.  For the upper bound, bound every
segment length by the total downstream path, every secant by
$\sec^{2}\theta_{\max}$, and every derivative by $D_{\max}$.  The
value of $D$ at zero angles follows from
$D_m=n_{m^-}\cos\theta_{m^-}/(n_{m^+}\cos\theta_{m^+})
=n_{m^-}/n_{m^+}$, whose product over the entry--exit pair of one
prism is $(1/n_{\mathrm{g}})\cdot n_{\mathrm{g}}=1$.
\end{proof}
The campaign measures the actual decay of median
$|c_{\vect{e}_i}|$ with $P$ (\PH{E5}).

\subsection{The feasibility frontier}

\begin{theorem}[Feasibility scaling]
\label{thm:frontier}
Let $T^{*}(P,\delta)$ be the least observation time at which a random
configuration satisfies conditions (i) and (iii) of
Definition~\ref{def:spectral} with probability at least $1-\delta$.
For a fixed box and fixed working orders $(B,K)$,
\begin{equation}
  T^{*}(P,\delta)=\bigO\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{\delta}P^{\max(2,K)}\Bigr)
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  T^{*}(P,\delta)=\Omega\!\bigl(P^{2}\bigr).
  \label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound is
Corollaries~\ref{cor:crowdT} and~\ref{cor:relT} with the union bound
over the two conditions.  For the lower bound, take
$s=\lambda L/P^{2}$ in~\eqref{eq:crowd}: the survival probability
tends to $e^{-\lambda}$, so with probability bounded away from zero
$g_{\mathrm{pair}}\le\lambda L/P^{2}$, and the merge floor
(Prop.~\ref{prop:pair}) converts this to
$T\ge c\,C_{\mathrm{m}}P^{2}/(\lambda L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Empirical frontier exponent]
\label{conj:frontier}
On the campaign box the measured frontier follows
$T^{*}\propto P^{\gamma}$ with
$2\le\gamma\le K$; we conjecture the crowding exponent $\gamma=2$
governs the practical range $P\le6$, with relations taking over only
beyond it.  E5 fits $\gamma=\PH{E5 fit}$.
\end{conjecture}

Two statements are deliberately left as measurements rather than
theorems.  First, recovery probability should be a function of the
\emph{margin coordinates} (pair gap, relation gap, minimal wedge)
alone, with $P$ acting only through their distributions---a collapse
prediction the atlas tests directly (\PH{E1/E5 collapse}).  Second,
full column rank of the $(4P+6)$-column completion Jacobian at
generic recovered points is verified per instance by the campaign
(\PH{E1: rank statistics}); we conjecture, but do not prove, that it
holds for almost every configuration at every $P$.

\section{The Large-Scale Computational Campaign}
\label{sec:campaign}
% =====================================================================

The statistical evidence for this paper is a computational campaign
sized to the claims: order $10^{6}$ random configurations under an
adaptive observation protocol, order $10^{7}$ ground-truth certificate
checks, and a prism-count frontier to $P{=}6$, executed as
embarrassingly parallel single-core tasks (estimated 50--100
core-days).  \textbf{Every yellow-boxed value and every framed figure
in this section is a placeholder}, specified in advance of the runs;
the analysis scripts that will fill them
(\texttt{experiments/sweep.py}, \texttt{experiments/aggregate.py})
are frozen in the repository together with the exact statistical
procedures described below, so the campaign is pre-registered in the
strict sense: the plots and tables were designed before the data
existed.

\subsection{Design and provenance}
\label{sec:design}

\emph{Case stream.}  Configuration $i\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ is generated
by a counter-based pseudorandom generator keyed by
$(\mathrm{seed}_0,i)$, uniform over the admissible box with
$|N_i|\ge\nu$.  Generation is \emph{order-free}: any shard can produce
any index, so the ensemble is defined by the integer range alone, and
adding shards never changes existing cases.  Results are append-only
records, one per (configuration, condition), resume-safe by key.

\emph{Margins at truth.}  For every configuration the identifiability
coordinates of Definition~\ref{def:spectral} are computed from ground
truth and stored with the record: minimal pair separation
$g_{\mathrm{pair}}$, minimal relation gap $g_{\mathrm{rel}}$ at order
$K$, minimal wedge magnitude $\min_i|\alpha_{x,i}|$, and the cycle
count of the slowest prism.  Every rate reported below can therefore
be resolved against the margins the theory says govern it---the
difference between measuring a phase diagram and quoting an average.

\emph{Adaptive protocol.}  The unit of evaluation is the protocol,
not a fixed $T$: solve at $T{=}10$\,s; on failure, follow the
certificate's prescription up the ladder $T{=}20,40,80$\,s.  Success
is $\max_i|\hat\theta_i-\theta_i^{*}|<10^{-3}$ (achieved errors are
typically $10^{-11}$; the threshold only classifies).

\emph{Statistics.}  All rates carry 95\% Wilson intervals; binned
margin curves require $\ge$20 configurations per bin; exponent fits
are ordinary least squares on $\log$--$\log$ medians with bootstrap
confidence intervals; nothing is truncated silently (any bounded
sub-analysis states its bound).

\emph{Arithmetic.}  Single-threaded pinned BLAS throughout:
multithreaded eigensolvers are not bitwise reproducible, and the
CLEAN greedy amplifies the difference on boundary cases.

\subsection{E7: baselines under identical conditions}
\label{sec:baselines}

The method is scored against three baselines on a common
$10^{4}$-configuration subset of the atlas: the FFT peak-picking
front end (magnitudes only; sign-blind by construction) that anchored
all previous approaches, and our two strongest predecessor solvers.
Since one of those is machine-learned we specify its design
completely: it is a serious, physics-informed architecture, and its
failure mode is what motivated the present method.

\emph{Staged neural solver.}  The learned solver factors the map along
the same physical hierarchy as the lattice method.  Stage~1 takes
speed magnitudes from the top-$P$ FFT peaks and resolves the $2^{P}$
signs by screening every combination through the forward model seeded
with stage-2 angles.  Stage~2 is a fully connected network
$28\!\to\!256\!\to\!256\!\to\!128\!\to\!6$ (batch normalization, GELU)
mapping the three signed speeds plus a 25-dimensional spectral feature
vector---top-3 peak frequencies, per-axis peak amplitudes,
$\sin/\cos$ of the peak phases, pattern centroid and spread---to the
six box-normalized wedge angles through a sigmoid head.  Stage~3
predicts the remaining nine parameters (glass, geometry, beam) by
fusing a four-stage strided 1-D convolutional encoder over the raw
$2\times200$ trace (channels $2\!\to\!32\!\to\!64\!\to\!128\!\to\!128$,
kernels $7/5/3/3$, global average pooling) with a conditioning branch
on the 34 previously determined quantities.  Both networks were
trained on $10^{6}$ simulated patterns ($5\times10^{4}$ held out)
drawn uniformly from the admissible box, with teacher-forced
conditioning: AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, one-cycle schedule, batch 512, gradient clipping, 80 and
100 epochs respectively.  The stage-3 loss augments parameter
mean-square error with a physics-informed term---pattern MSE through
a differentiable port of the forward model (validated to
$2\times10^{-6}$ against the exact model), weight $0.1$, evaluated
every fourth batch.  At inference the prediction receives 300 Adam
refinement steps through the differentiable model before the same
trust-region completion every method in the comparison shares.

Its failure rate (\PH{E7}) is structural, not capacity- or
data-limited.  First, stage~1 inherits every pathology of the FFT
front end: harmonics and
cross-terms displace fundamentals among the top peaks in exactly the
hard cases, corrupting the conditioning features downstream.  Second,
the completion basin is 1--5$^{\circ}$ wide in wedge angle (Test~A7)
while the angle posterior conditioned on corrupted peaks is
multimodal; a regression network returns a compromise between modes
that lies in no basin.  More data or parameters removes neither
obstruction; replacing learned initialization with the exact lattice
coordinates removes both.

\emph{Grid solver.}  The strongest pre-lattice method replaces the
networks with enumeration: candidate speed triples from the top-12
FFT peaks, ranked per triple by a coarse $5^{3}$ forward screen; for
each of the best five triples, a $9^{3}$ joint grid over the three
$\alpha_x$ (with $\alpha_y=0$; the basin is wide in $\alpha_y$,
narrow in $\alpha_x$), screened through the differentiable model,
best nodes finished by trust region.  Its failures are the FFT front
end failing, not the grid: an oracle-speed control (identical grid,
true speeds supplied) isolates the front end's share of the failure
rate (\PH{E7: oracle control}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Relation to prior methods}

The spectral stage assembles classical components---Prony-family line
estimation~\cite{HuaSarkar1990,RoyKailath1989}, CLEAN
deconvolution~\cite{Hogbom1974}, variable
projection~\cite{GolubPereyra1973}, and the parsimony principle of
integer-relation finding~\cite{LLL1982}---around one problem-specific
observation: for Risley systems the unknown frequencies are not free
but lattice-generated, and the physically meaningful unknowns are the
\emph{generators}.  This lattice-generator viewpoint---that the spectrum
of a quasi-periodic signal is the integer-lattice image of a few
fundamental frequencies, recovered as generators rather than by peak
picking---is the organizing principle of Laskar's frequency-map analysis
(NAFF)~\cite{Laskar1990,Laskar1993} in Hamiltonian and accelerator
dynamics; what is specific to Risley identification is that the
generators are the \emph{signed} rotation speeds, that the
fundamental-line phases and amplitudes are the wedge orientations and
magnitudes through the Snell readout of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}, and that
generator selection is made a decidable arithmetic problem
(Lemma~\ref{lem:finer}, the integer-coverage score) rather than left to
resonance inspection.  Treating extraction as generator identification
(rather than peak picking) is what eliminates, simultaneously, the
harmonic-confusion failure mode, the sign enumeration, and the
frequency-triple search.  The completion stage then inherits a
well-posed local problem, consistent with the identifiability analysis.

Our own earlier pipeline (FFT peaks, neural initialization, staged
descent; Sec.~\ref{sec:baselines}) reached the same terminal precision
\emph{when} its initialization landed in the basin, but required a
$56\times8$-candidate screen and a $9^3$ grid to get there, at
$\sim\!35$\,min per hard case and roughly half reliability in the 9-D
protocol (E7).  The neural initializer---a physics-informed staged design
trained on $10^{6}$ patterns, not a strawman---is superseded outright:
the lattice coordinates make the hard part of the map nearly linear,
and nothing remains for a network to learn at $P{=}3$.

\subsection{Negative results with content}

Three predecessor designs of the spectral stage failed during
development, and each failure is a reusable lesson now embedded in the
theory: residual-guided greedy basis growth is structurally unsound at
lattice order $\ge2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:finer}); unregularized amplitude
solves explode on near-coincident lines while plain ridge
\emph{splits} them (hence merge + ridge + sharp-final); and
re-extracting fundamentals from refined fits harvests the splitting
artifacts (hence extract once, from the selection fit only).  We record
these because they are exactly the traps a re-implementation would
fall into.

Twice, moreover, development-scale ensemble testing (order
$10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ configurations) falsified the \emph{completeness}
of the failure taxonomy---never a theorem, but the implicit claim
that every failure had a name---and each time the resolution was a
sharper theory: one ensemble exposed the merge-floor term missing
from $T_{\mathrm{req}}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:treq}], and a single
unexplained case in another (healthy amplitudes, clean separations,
no TIR; the truth-seeded fit converged to $10^{-25}$) forced
condition~(v) and the overload retry into
Definition~\ref{def:spectral}.  We regard this falsification
loop---taxonomy, counterexample, derived condition---as part of the
method, and the campaign's zero-unexplained target (E1) is its
continuation at three orders of magnitude larger scale.

\subsection{Limitations}

The certificates make the limitations quantitative rather than
anecdotal: pair separations below $\sim\!3\sigma(T)$, wedge angles
below the stated detectability threshold, exact lattice relations, and
TIR-dominated traces are unrecoverable \emph{at the given $T$}---each
with a computable $T_{\mathrm{req}}$, validated at scale by E4.
Beyond these: the per-axis reduced Snell model is exact only for
the self-consistent inversion (against real hardware the
$\bigO(\alpha_x\alpha_y)$ cross-coupling would enter as model
mismatch); the completion stage is implemented for $P{=}3$, its
$P$-generalization being mechanical (the readout laws are per-prism)
but not yet executed; certificate prism indices refer to the fitted
basis and can misalign when that basis is wrong (the run-time
feasible-yet-failed self-report remains the detector); cure-time
composition for multi-deficit configurations is not derived---only
the single-deficit scalings of Sec.~\ref{sec:certify} are; global
(as opposed to local) uniqueness of the non-paraxial inverse remains
open; the campaign is specified and pre-registered but its data are
pending (every dependent value is boxed); and this is a simulation
study---experimental validation on physical hardware, where the
certificate framework should absorb measurement noise as in E6, is
the natural next step.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

The multi-prism Risley inverse problem is solved by recognizing its
algebraic skeleton: the scan pattern lives on the frequency lattice of
the signed speeds, the wedge angles are the phases and amplitudes of
the fundamental lines, and the remaining parameters are pinned by one
well-initialized trust-region solve on the exact model.  The resulting
inversion recovers the complete $4P+6$-parameter specification from a
single pattern with no enumeration and no training, to near machine
precision, in seconds---and, we believe more importantly, it
\emph{knows what it knows}: every output is accompanied by an error
bound whose calibration is testable, or by a quantitative statement
of why recovery is impossible at the given observation time, with a
prescription for how much observation would suffice.  The scaling
theory of Sec.~\ref{sec:scaling} extends the guarantees to arbitrary
prism count---polynomial cost, $\Theta(P^{2})$ crowding demand,
degree-$K$ relation demand, and a feasibility frontier---and the
pre-registered campaign of Sec.~\ref{sec:campaign} subjects every
statistical claim, including the frontier exponents and the
zero-unexplained-failure target, to tests at the $10^{6}$-configuration
scale.

All code (the \texttt{risley\_lattice} package), the campaign harness
and every assumption test (\texttt{experiments/sweep.py},
\texttt{aggregate.py}, A1--A8 scripts), and the raw result records
