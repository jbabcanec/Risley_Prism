# Collision recovery experiment, 2026-10-02

The original passive 18-parameter exact-speed-collision development case is
numerically recovered by an observation-only program to maximum native
coordinate error **1.35e-9**, using the original 200 samples over 10 seconds.
This is a concrete recovery of all 18 unknowns, not a fixed-speed or known-glass
fit. Two later, separately generated blind collision holdouts also recover all
18 coordinates with the byte-identical frozen solver. Three examples do not
establish a global recovery theorem or a population success rate.

## What changed

The standard spectral front end extracted two strong lines at approximately
`-0.71000286` and `1.19996594`. Asking it for three physical speeds produced a
spurious third speed (`2.40065` with FFT; `1.28935` with matrix pencil).

The existing repeated-generator routine already forms duplicate-speed hypotheses
and releases all three speeds after fitting. Its starts predominantly split a
line into reinforcing contributions. On this example the repeated-frequency
complex coefficient is approximately `-1.255 + 1.507i`. Its phase is outside
the allowed single-prism signed phase cones in the initializer's leading-order
phasor model. Opposing contributions can produce that coefficient while each
prism phase remains in the native interval. This motivates a search hypothesis;
it is not an exact spectral test proving the presence of a collision.

`recover.py` therefore solves the two-by-two real system

    a*cos(phi1) + b*cos(phi2) = Re(c)
    a*sin(phi1) + b*sin(phi2) = Im(c)

for signed contributions at phase pairs `(-16,16)`, `(16,-16)`, `(-8,8)`,
`(8,-8)` degrees. It also keeps four signed in-phase allocations. It repeats
each of the two strongest independent observed generators, places the singleton
in each of the three physical positions, and screens all 48 starts. Existing
exact bounded affine variable projection estimates workpiece distance, common
gap and both beam positions; those four remain unknown, not fixed.

The policy screens 24 evaluations per start, refines the best 12 with up to
400 repeated-relation evaluations followed by 400 unrestricted evaluations,
then applies the existing canonical full18 `trf` polish to the lowest-MSE
candidate. No simulated truth determines candidate ranking or stopping.
The final fit has all three speeds free. No controlled measurements are used.

same frozen policy; they do not start from a noise-free answer. This waveform
is one empirical test, not a worst-case robustness claim. The least-squares
candidate's max residual slightly exceeds the injected amplitude, so these
runs are not hard-band feasibility certificates. `evaluation.json` contains
truth, estimate and absolute error separately for every coordinate and run.

## Exact scope and limitations

The canonical reduced per-axis model, source distance 6, thickness 3, and native
box in `risley_lattice/model.py` are retained. All 18 coordinates are unknown.
No minimum wedge, known speed, sorted physical order or restricted glass range
is imposed on the final fit. The routine proposes same-signed repeated speed
hypotheses; it is not exhaustive for arbitrary distinct speeds, opposite-signed
collisions or every possible low-frequency spectrum. Its finite starts and
residual selection give no exclusion of remote compatible systems.

The researcher accidentally saw the synthetic stress truth while reading
`contract.py` for the benchmark contract, and disclosed that before coding.
The inverse scripts themselves read only `observations.json`; no true values
enter their data-derived or generic grid initializers. This is therefore an
observation-only program on a development example, not evidence that the human
research process was never exposed to truth. `evaluate.py` reads `cases.json`
only after candidate files are saved.
