# Conditional audit: two-chart subresultant reconstruction of the last index

## Status

This note audits only the proposed implication from a future exact witness certificate. It does not assert that the two-norm certificate has been obtained. No existing audited memo is modified by this conditional argument.

The implication is sound. The cleanest proof uses two fixed ungrouped formal norm polynomials and the universal division-free first-subresultant identity. Grouping repeated radicals remains useful for arbitrary-point fallback computations but is unnecessary, and less convenient, for this generic analytic argument.

## 1. Fixed coefficient functions and the proposed certificate

Let x denote the retained thirteen optical coordinates, let y denote the entire observed screen record, and let G=n₃². Use X as a separate polynomial indeterminate. Choose the fixed five-row charts (0,1,2,3,4) and (0,1,2,3,5).

For each chart, form its augmented sagittal determinant using formal radicals H_i with H_i²=X−|q_i|². Take the product over all 32 independent formal sign choices and reduce even powers. This gives fixed polynomials

    P(X;x,y), Q(X;x,y),

of degree at most 64 in X. Their coefficients are analytic semialgebraic functions of the retained optical coordinates and y, with the selected physical upstream radicals included in their evaluation. Their formal coefficient formulas remain analytic when some |q_i|² values coincide. At such specializations an ungrouped norm can become the zero polynomial; that is allowed here.

For every physical full system theta, evaluated at y=F(theta), the positive-radical determinant factor vanishes at X=G(theta). Consequently

    P(G(theta);x(theta),F(theta))=0,
    Q(G(theta);x(theta),F(theta))=0

identically throughout the physical interior. This remains true for repeated-radicand specializations and artificial conjugate factors.

The proposed certificate at the exact rational-rotor witness is:

1. Both leading coefficients at degree 64 are nonzero.
2. With G*=9/4, divide the exact specialized polynomials by X−G*.
3. The degree-63 quotient polynomials have nonzero resultant.

The exact root in step 2 follows from the optical identity, not merely from an interval containing zero. The witness data must mean the exact algebraic screen positions generated by the specified physical system, rather than rounded decimal substitutes.

If the three conditions are certified, then

    gcd(P*,Q*)=X−G*

up to a nonzero constant. Indeed, both share X−G*, and their quotients are coprime. The leading-coefficient conditions ensure that the degree-64 subresultant specialization is degree-preserving.

## 2. Universal subresultant identity

Form the division-free first subresultant for the fixed degree bounds (64,64):

    S₁(X)=a(x,y) X+b(x,y).

Its coefficients are polynomial expressions in the coefficients of P and Q. It has a universal Bezout representation

    S₁(X)=U(X)P(X)+V(X)Q(X),

with coefficient expressions supplied by the subresultant determinant construction. Use this polynomial identity directly, rather than a Euclidean implementation that divides by possibly vanishing intermediate coefficients.

Therefore, at every physical system,

    a(x(theta),F(theta)) G(theta)+b(x(theta),F(theta))=0.

This identity does not require the norm polynomials to remain degree 64 at every system. Degree preservation is needed at the witness to infer that a is nonzero there from its certified degree-one gcd. At that witness the first subresultant is a nonzero constant multiple of X−G*, so a≠0.

It follows that wherever a≠0,

    G=−b/a,
    n₃=positive_sqrt(−b/a).

The result reconstructs the squared index rationally in the norm coefficients. It does not assert that n₃ itself is rational.

## 3. Coefficient-field interpretation

The operational proof above does not require a separate irreducibility theorem. If a field statement is desired, its coefficient field must exclude G as an initially supplied coordinate.

Take the field generated by the retained thirteen optical chart coordinates, the observed y coordinates, and the selected physical upstream auxiliary radicals/directions/transport coefficients. The norm coefficients belong to this field. The subresultant formula establishes that G belongs to the same field generically.

This is rationality in an explicitly enlarged coefficient field, not necessarily rationality in the bare retained native angles and observations, and certainly not a rational inverse from y alone. All upstream auxiliary quantities are computable from the retained optical coordinates on their known physical branch and introduce no additional searched optical coordinate.

For completeness, the relevant graph and coefficient-image Zariski closures are irreducible over R. Pull a polynomial relation back to the connected real-analytic physical parameter domain O. If a product f·g vanishes identically, and f is not identically zero, f is nonzero on a nonempty open subset; g vanishes there and hence everywhere by analytic continuation. Thus the vanishing ideal is prime. The same argument applies after projecting to the coefficient coordinates. Semialgebraicity supplies the finite-dimensional algebraic/semialgebraic framework. None of this requires interpreting all independently signed norm factors as physical rays.

## 4. Whole-prior generic observation statement

Use the established connected eighteen-dimensional physical interior O, compact weak closure W, continuous semialgebraic extension Fbar, and dim(W\\O)≤17. After the proposed witness succeeds, the function

    A(theta)=a(x(theta),F(theta))

is analytic semialgebraic on O and nonzero somewhere. Consequently

    Z={theta in O:A(theta)=0}

has dimension at most 17. Define

    B=(W\\O) union closure_W(Z),
    E=Fbar(B).

The set E is compact semialgebraic and has dimension at most 17. The existing full18 rank theorem makes the observation image eighteen-dimensional, so E is lower-dimensional in that image.

For every exact y in Fbar(W) outside E, all compatible full systems are interior and have A≠0. Thus at every compatible retained-thirteen-coordinate optical point the formula G=−b/a recovers the unique possible squared index. Different full solutions can still have different retained optical coordinates. This does not establish uniqueness of the complete optical system.

Only the zero set of A is needed for this generic exclusion: additional leading-coefficient exclusions are unnecessary once the universal identity and a≠0 apply. Adding them is harmless if their nonvanishing is separately witnessed.

At arbitrary input data, a=0 must remain a fallback case unless the observation has been validly certified outside E. Nonzero a does not establish full-model feasibility: the reconstructed index must satisfy the original index prior, the complete exact observation equations, the geometry constraints, and all physical branch/traversal tests. The denominator may be arbitrarily small near the exceptional set; no uniform conditioning or practical complexity conclusion follows.

## 5. Physical roots versus norm conjugates

The two individual norm polynomials can contain nonphysical conjugate roots, artificial roots from ungrouped repeated radicals, and roots that do not support compatible geometry. These do not invalidate the subresultant identity, since the true physical G is always a common root.

At points with a≠0, every common root of the two evaluated norm polynomials must satisfy aX+b=0 by the same Bezout identity. Thus there is at most one distinct common root there, and any compatible physical index must be it. This conditional uniqueness is stronger than separately filtering two finite root lists, but still concerns only the eliminated index conditional on x and y.

No claim in this note becomes an established theorem until the degree-preserving gcd-one witness certificate has actually been completed and checked.
